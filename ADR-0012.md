# ADR-0012: Prepaid pay-as-you-go for OpenEntitlement Cloud

- **Status:** accepted commercial direction; billing architecture proposed and delivery not implemented by this decision. No protocol change.
- **Date:** 2026-11-16
- **Builds on:** [VISION](VISION.md), [ADR-0001](ADR-0001.md), [ADR-0004](ADR-0004.md) and [ADR-0008](ADR-0008.md).

## Decision

OpenEntitlement Cloud uses prepaid, funded usage (prepaid PAYG). Customers fund monetary credits before consuming paid Cloud service usage. Credits become available only after confirmed payment; initiating checkout, an invoice or a pending payment is insufficient. Automatic recharge is optional, with customer opt-in; it must not create spendable credits before payment confirmation.

Aim for the lowest sustainable price, passing the cost of delivering and billing the service to customers with a modest reserve. The cost basis includes infrastructure, storage and backups, network, database, observability and operations, metering and billing overhead (for example a Metronome adapter), and payment processing fees. Validate actual unit costs and allocations before publishing rates. This selects a commercial model, not a rate card, minimum purchase or fixed margin.

Prepayment limits credit exposure; it does not guarantee zero losses. Payment reversals, delayed measurement, concurrent usage, abuse and shared operating costs can still produce losses. The reserve and operational controls must be informed by observed costs, rather than a promise that every customer or request is profitable.

This selects prepaid usage over default billing in arrears. A flat subscription or enterprise commitment is not selected by this decision. Metronome is a potential billing/metering adapter, not a mandatory vendor commitment. A payment processor and adapter implementation remain to be selected and validated.

## Boundaries and language

This decision applies only to OpenEntitlement Cloud's own charges. The portable protocol, application-facing SDKs and self-hosted behavior are unaffected. It does not introduce a customer-application billing engine or change Cloud capability packaging.

Monetary credits are prepaid Cloud service value, distinct from an OpenEntitlement **Grant** (`OEGrant`), which adds an entitlement allowance. Neither a Cloud balance nor a billing vendor's credit grant becomes an OEGrant. An Organization's Minimum commitment remains display-only under [ADR-0011](ADR-0011.md).

Pure evaluation remains a read: it must not consume application quota, charge a payment or allocate an external license. If Cloud requests are priced, Cloud request usage is measured outside the pure evaluator by the hosting/billing boundary. Such service usage is separate from the calling application's usage; evaluations are not evidence that an application action occurred. No evaluation side effect or wire change is authorized here.

## Proposed architecture and open decisions

The following are recommendations for a future RFC, not accepted mechanics or implementation requirements. Rates, schemas, APIs, rollout and delivery tickets need separate agreement.

| Open decision | Recommended starting point (proposed) |
|---|---|
| Billing aggregation | Bill the Workspace (already the customer in ADR-0008), aggregate its Products and show a per-Product breakdown. A shared monetary balance and aggregation details still need approval. |
| Metering units | Benchmark Cloud requests and persistent resource costs first; prefer a small understandable set of units and publish their definitions before charging. Do not use application quota consumption as a proxy. |
| Bulk, retries and denials | Compare per-request with per-item charging for bulk; recommend per-item accounting with an explicit cap/size policy. Deduplicate internal delivery retries. Define client retry identity/windows separately. Recommend no charge for authentication/validation failures or service errors; explicitly decide whether a successfully evaluated entitlement denial counts as Cloud service work. |
| Ledger and payment confirmation | Recommend an auditable monetary ledger, idempotent payment/event identities, verified payment notifications and reconciliation; settle confirmation/finality and reversal handling before implementation. |
| Balance enforcement and concurrency | Recommend bounded reservations at the Cloud boundary with settlement/release, rather than relying only on delayed low-balance notifications. Decide reservation expiry, crash recovery, partial bulk results, overdraft tolerance and billing-outage behavior. |
| Exhausted balance | Recommend stopping new paid Cloud work and notifying the customer, while preserving access to billing/recharge controls. Specify recovery and error semantics separately; do not reinterpret an entitlement denial as a billing failure. |
| Currency and precision | Recommend one explicit billing currency initially and integer minor-unit accounting with defined rounding. Currency, conversion, tax treatment and price-version timing remain open. |
| Recharge and purchase sizes | Recommend explicit opt-in, visible thresholds and spending limits. Minimum funding, recharge amounts, retry limits and failed-payment behavior remain open. |
| Refunds, expiry and reversals | Recommend explicit customer terms and traceable compensating ledger entries. Refund eligibility, expiry, transferability, disputes and chargebacks require separate decisions; do not infer non-refundable or expiring credits. |
| Cost allocation and disclosure | Recommend validating shared/fixed cost allocation, idle storage costs and billing event volume as well as request costs. Publish understandable rates and usage freshness; private operational cost findings stay private. |

For example, a Workspace with Products A and B could fund one balance and see separate A/B service charges under the aggregation recommendation. An entitlement check for an Organization in A would still resolve its Privileges without decrementing that Organization's application allowance. An Account in another Workspace would have no access to this balance. This illustrates the proposal, not a delivered balance API.

## Evidence and consequences

The [Metronome research note](docs/research/cloud-prepaid-billing.md) compares the primary billing-model guides. Their terminology and example policies are evidence, not adopted OpenEntitlement contracts. Detailed architecture belongs in a future RFC; this ADR settles only the commercial direction and preserves the evaluation boundary.

Delivery remains planned in [PRD](PRD.md). No billing accounts, payments, integration setup, ledger or metering implementation are introduced by this documentation change.
